If shared blind spots were just growing pains, newer models would fix them. Mostly they don't — and an upgrade hides new ones almost twice as often as it inherits old ones (most of the newly hidden were already wrong; the models just stopped disagreeing).

Eight models answered the identical corpus, so every subset of them is an ensemble you could have deployed.

154 of them, scored for nothing. The question this tab answers is the one a practitioner actually faces: given a budget of two, three or four models, which ones should they be? The answer is not "the most accurate".

Blind spots track correlation, not capability?

Each dot is one ensemble. Among ensembles of the same size, the blind-spot rate is almost entirely predicted by how correlated the models are and barely at all by how accurate they are. Switch the vertical axis to see the cost: correlation buys detection at the same time it costs coverage.

2025 models only, so no generation is mixed in. The bars are the spread across ensembles of that size, not a confidence interval.

Where the vote is blind, the record is not?

On the questions where all four models agreed, disagreement is zero by construction. Answer only the ones the record is surest about, and the error rate among what you keep falls well below the rate you get by answering everything.
